The challenge

Why store ads stop being profitable.

Getting a few sales is easy. Keeping ROAS when the budget grows is the real job.

ROAS that dies when you scale

Doubling the budget on one ad set usually raises the cost per purchase. Scaling needs more winning ads, not only more money.

Too few creatives

One or two ads fatigue fast. Stores that keep testing new creatives are the ones that keep a steady cost per purchase.

Broken tracking

If the pixel and purchase events are wrong, Meta optimizes on bad data and ROAS in Ads Manager can't be trusted.

Margin ignored

A 3× ROAS can be a loss on one product and great on another. Targets have to match your margin and average order value.

FAQ

E-commerce ads: common questions.

What does an e-commerce media buyer do?

An e-commerce media buyer runs paid sales campaigns, mainly on Meta, that bring purchases to an online store. That includes checking tracking, testing creatives, structuring campaigns, managing budgets and scaling while keeping ROAS and cost per purchase on target.

What is a good ROAS for an online store?

It depends on your margin. A store with high margins can be profitable at a lower ROAS than one selling low-margin products. In the accounts on this page, ROAS ranged from 12.53× across 86 ads to 22.51× over a 13-day period.

Do I need a website, or can I sell through WhatsApp?

Both can work. Website purchase campaigns give clear ROAS tracking; messaging campaigns suit stores that close sales in chat. Several retail and pharmacy accounts on this site sell through conversations.

How many ads should I run?

More than most stores think. The apparel account on this page ran 86 ads, and a handful of them carried most of the sales. Testing is how you find those winners.

Which platforms do you use for e-commerce?

Mainly Meta (Facebook and Instagram), plus TikTok, Snapchat and Google Ads depending on the product and audience.
